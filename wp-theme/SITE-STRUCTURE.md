# CDMG Website: Structure and Why It Works

A plain-language explanation of how the new site is organized, why it is built
this way, and why it is easier to use than a traditional layout. Written so it
can be shared with the team and with stakeholders.

---

## The big picture

The site is built around two ideas working together:

1. **A guided homepage that tells the whole story in one downward scroll.** A
   visitor who does nothing but scroll the homepage gets the complete message,
   in order, from "who we are" to "let's talk." They never have to hunt through
   menus to understand CDMG.
2. **A small set of focused pages for people who want to go deeper.** The
   methodology, the verticals, the company story, the blog, and the contact
   form each get their own page, reachable from the top menu, for the visitor
   who wants detail on one subject.

This is a deliberate combination. The homepage carries the narrative so nobody
gets lost. The focused pages hold the depth so the homepage never becomes
cluttered. A note on terms: the homepage is the long scroll. The rest of the
site is a few clean pages off the menu. That pairing is the structure, and it
is what makes the site both easy to skim and easy to dig into.

---

## Why this is more user-friendly than a traditional site

Older marketing sites tend to scatter the message across many pages and force
the visitor to click around to assemble the story. That creates friction, and
friction loses people.

The new structure fixes that in three ways:

- **One clear path.** The homepage is sequenced like a conversation: attention,
  proof, method, results, then the ask. The visitor is carried through it.
- **No dead ends.** Every section ends by either making a point or offering a
  next step, so momentum always moves toward "Let's Talk."
- **Less thinking required.** People do not have to decide where to click to
  understand the business. Scrolling is the most natural motion on the web, and
  the homepage rewards it.

## The homepage, section by section

Each block on the homepage has a job. Here is what each one does and why it is
placed where it is.

1. **Hero with the awards badge and consultation form.** The first screen states
   the promise ("the art of marketing meets the science of sales"), proves
   credibility instantly with "100+ Marketing Awards Won," and puts a
   consultation form right there so a ready visitor can act without scrolling.
   Lead capture at the top captures the people who are already convinced.
2. **Client logo strip.** Immediately after the promise, recognizable brands
   build trust through association. Seeing known names lowers the visitor's
   guard before any selling happens.
3. **Value proposition (Accountable Advertising).** Three cards translate the
   promise into concrete benefits: tested messaging, multi-channel delivery,
   measurable profit. This answers "what do I actually get."
4. **5 Dimensional Marketing cards.** This introduces the proprietary method at
   a glance, with a card that invites the visitor to the full methodology page.
   It signals depth without forcing it.
5. **Stats band.** Four numbers (40+ years, 100+ awards, 126+ founder awards, 7x
   growth) give fast, scannable proof of scale and longevity.
6. **Why CDMG split.** A focused list of what working with CDMG delivers,
   paired with the core promise quote. This is the "why us" moment.
7. **Process steps (Discover, Build and Launch, Measure and Scale).** Shows the
   engagement is simple and structured, which reduces the fear of "what would
   working together even look like."
8. **Testimonials.** Social proof in the client's own voice, placed after the
   method and process so it confirms claims the visitor has just read.
9. **Selected work gallery.** Breadth of categories served, reinforcing that the
   approach travels across industries.
10. **FAQ.** Answers the common hesitations directly, which also feeds search
    and answer engines (the questions are structured for that).
11. **Blog teaser (latest posts).** Shows the company is active and generous with
    expertise, and pulls the three newest articles automatically so the homepage
    always looks current.
12. **Closing call to action.** The scroll ends where it should: an invitation
    to talk. By here the visitor has the full case and a clear next step.

The order is intentional. It moves from promise, to proof, to method, to
results, to action. That is how a good salesperson talks, and the page mirrors
it.

## Why the long scroll is especially better on mobile

More than half of visitors arrive on a phone, and the scrolling homepage is
built for them.

- **Scrolling beats tapping.** On a small screen, swiping down is effortless,
  while hunting through a menu and tapping tiny links is not. The homepage asks
  for the easy motion.
- **One column, in order.** On a phone every section stacks into a single,
  readable column in the intended sequence, so the story stays intact. (This is
  also why we recently made the methodology blocks stack with the colored panel
  above each title, so they read cleanly top to bottom.)
- **The form is never far.** Because the page is one continuous scroll, the
  visitor is always a short swipe from a way to contact you.
- **Fast and light.** The design avoids heavy page-to-page reloads for the core
  story, which matters most on mobile connections.

## The focused pages

Off the homepage, the menu leads to clean, single-subject pages:

- **5 Dimensions** explains the methodology in full.
- **Why You** covers the verticals and the two kinds of growth CDMG drives.
- **Our Story** tells the history (founded in California in 1985, now in
  Nashville) and introduces the team.
- **Contact** is the dedicated place to reach the team.

Keeping these separate is what lets the homepage stay focused. Someone who wants
the deep methodology can get all of it without the homepage having to carry it.

## The DMU Blog: organized by topic, findable by subject

This is where your library of expertise lives, and it is built to make a large
archive easy to navigate.

- **Articles are grouped by topic using categories.** Each article belongs to a
  subject (for example Direct Response Copy Tips, Digital Marketing, Test
  Results, Case Studies). Your existing categories carry straight over.
- **Browse by topic is built in.** The blog page shows a "Browse by topic" row
  of your most-used subjects. Clicking one opens a page of every article on that
  subject, in the same design, with a count of how many there are.
- **Every category tag is clickable.** On any card or article, the subject tag
  is a link. A reader who likes one article on, say, direct mail can click the
  tag and instantly see all of them.
- **Search by topic or keyword.** There is a search box on the blog. A visitor
  can type a subject or phrase and get every matching article. So yes, the blog
  is fully searchable by topic, both by clicking a subject and by typing one.
- **The newest article is featured.** The most recent post is highlighted at the
  top of the blog automatically, so the page always leads with fresh thinking.

The result: a visitor can land on the blog and either follow a topic they care
about, search for a specific subject, or simply scroll the latest. Thousands of
articles stay findable instead of disappearing into endless pages.

## Your archive: ready, and already in place

This is the part worth understanding clearly.

- **You have 2,029 published articles**, with the earliest from **October
  2014**. That is roughly twelve years of continuously published expertise.
- **Nothing needs to be "moved."** The new design is a theme applied to the same
  WordPress that already holds these articles. The moment the theme is active,
  all 2,029 articles take on the new look, keep their categories, and flow into
  the topic browsing and search described above. No re-creating, no risk of
  losing anything. On WP Engine, the staging copy includes the full archive too,
  so it can all be previewed before going live.
- **About touting your history.** The company's roots go back to 1985, and that
  story is told proudly on the Our Story page. The blog archive itself runs from
  2014 forward. So the honest and strong way to present it is: four decades in
  business, with a deep, searchable library of marketing wisdom built over the
  last twelve years. That is a genuine, defensible claim, and the archive backs
  it up.

## Why the whole structure is better, in one line

The site meets people the way they actually browse: a single, guided scroll that
tells the story and asks for the next step, focused pages for anyone who wants
depth, and a deep blog that stays findable by topic and search. It is easier to
skim, easier to navigate, and stronger on mobile than a traditional multi-page
layout, and it puts twelve years of your expertise to work.
