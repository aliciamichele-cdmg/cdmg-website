# Publishing a Blog Post: First Post Walkthrough

A step by step guide for publishing an AEO-optimized article on the DMU Blog.
No code required. Plan for about 15 minutes your first time, less after that.

"AEO" means Answer Engine Optimization: writing so that search engines and AI
answer tools can pull a clear, direct answer from your article and credit CDMG.
The two things that make that work are **clear structure** and **schema**, and
this theme plus Rank Math handle the schema for you.

---

## Before you start

- You are working in **WordPress** (on staging first, then live).
- **Rank Math SEO** is installed and active. If you do not see a "Rank Math"
  panel while editing, ask whoever set up the site to activate it.

---

## Step 1. Create the post

1. In the left menu, go to **Posts > Add New**.
2. You are now in the editor. The big field at the top is the **title**.

---

## Step 2. Write an AEO-friendly title

The title is the single most important line for answer engines.

- Make it a clear statement or a question a real person would type or ask.
- Put the main topic near the front.
- Keep it under about 60 characters so it does not get cut off.

Good: `What Is Direct Response Advertising? A Plain Guide`
Weak: `Some Thoughts on Marketing in Today's World`

---

## Step 3. Write the body with structure

Click below the title and start writing. Press Enter to create each new block.

A few rules that matter for AEO:

1. **Answer the main question in the first one or two sentences.** Do not bury
   it. If the title asks "What is X," the first paragraph should plainly say
   what X is.
2. **Use Heading blocks for each section.** Type `/heading` and press Enter, or
   use the block toolbar. Headings should read like questions or clear topics
   (for example, "How does it work?" or "Who is it for?"). Answer engines lift
   answers from the text right under a heading.
3. **Keep paragraphs short.** Two to four sentences each.
4. **Use bullet or numbered lists** for steps and key points. Type `/list`.
5. Write plainly and factually. Concrete, specific sentences get quoted more
   often than vague ones.

---

## Step 4. Add the FAQ dropdowns (this is the schema part)

This is what creates the collapsible question and answer dropdowns on the page,
and it automatically generates the FAQ schema that answer engines read.

1. At the spot where you want the FAQ section (usually near the end), add a new
   block: click the **+** button, search **FAQ**, and choose the **Rank Math
   FAQ block** (it may appear as "FAQ by Rank Math").
2. Type a **question** in the question field and its **answer** below it.
3. Click **+ Add New Question** to add more. Three to six is a good range.
4. Use real questions people ask about the topic. Keep answers short and direct,
   two or three sentences.

That is it. The dropdowns appear on the page styled to match the site, and the
FAQ schema is added behind the scenes with nothing else to do.

---

## Step 5. Set the category and featured image

On the right side panel (if you do not see it, click the gear icon top right):

1. **Category:** open **Categories**, check one (for example Strategy, Digital,
   Compliance, Data Strategy). This becomes the tag shown on the blog card and
   the article. Pick one main category per post.
2. **Featured image:** open **Featured image > Set featured image**, and upload
   or choose an image. This becomes the card image on the blog page and the
   banner on the article. A wide image works best, around 1200 by 800 pixels.

---

## Step 6. Fill in the Rank Math panel (the AEO checklist)

Find **Rank Math** in the right panel, or the Rank Math icon in the top bar.

1. **Focus Keyword:** type the main phrase the article should rank for (for
   example "direct response advertising"). Use it in the title, the first
   paragraph, and at least one heading.
2. **Edit Snippet:** check the **SEO Title** and write a **Description** of
   about one to two sentences that answers the topic. This is what shows in
   search results.
3. **Schema:** confirm the schema type is set to **Article**. The FAQ block you
   added contributes FAQ schema on top of that automatically.
4. Watch the **score** Rank Math shows (out of 100). Aim for green, in the 80s
   or higher. It lists specific suggestions you can fix as you go.

---

## Step 7. Preview, then publish

1. Click **Preview** (top right) to see the article in the real design. Check
   that the FAQ dropdowns open and close and the image looks right.
2. When it looks good, click **Publish**. To keep a weekly cadence, click the
   date next to "Publish" and **schedule** it for a future day instead.
3. Open the **DMU Blog** page. Your new post appears there automatically, no
   other step needed. The newest post becomes the featured article at the top.

---

## Quick pre-publish checklist

- [ ] Title is clear and includes the main topic
- [ ] First paragraph answers the main question directly
- [ ] Sections use Heading blocks, written as questions or clear topics
- [ ] An FAQ block with three to six real questions is included
- [ ] One Category is selected
- [ ] A Featured image is set
- [ ] Rank Math: focus keyword set, description written, schema is Article
- [ ] Rank Math score is green
- [ ] Previewed, dropdowns work, then Published or Scheduled

---

## If something looks off

The post content, FAQ blocks, and images are all yours to edit anytime by
reopening the post. If the **design or layout** of the blog itself needs a
change (something the editor cannot do), that is a theme change: send it to your
Claude Code contact and an updated theme file comes back to install.
